\section{Discover, Certify, and Repair}
\label{sec:discover-repair}

\subsection{Learning failure subgroups without invalidating inference}
Hand-designed group and score buckets are interpretable but can miss interactions among user history, item type, time, model confidence, and network position. Directly maximizing an empirical violation over a rich auditor class and reporting the same value as a certificate is invalid: the search overfits the noise, especially when inverse propensities are heavy-tailed.

\COMET\ uses a two-stage protocol. Split the window into graph--time separated discovery and certification sets. On the discovery set, fit an auditor
\begin{equation}
\widehat h
\in\argmax_{h\in\Hcal_{\mathrm{rich}}}
\abs{\widehat M_{\mathrm{disc}}(q,h)}
-\lambda\,\Omega(h),
\label{eq:auditor-learning}
\end{equation}
where $\Hcal_{\mathrm{rich}}$ may be a shallow tree, sparse rule list, boosted model, or neural auditor and $\Omega$ controls complexity or minimum support. Freeze $\widehat h$, then run \cref{alg:comet} on the certification split. Conditional on the discovery data, $\widehat h$ is a fixed test, so the certification guarantee does not depend on the complexity of the search model. Multiple discovered auditors can be certified by including the frozen top-$L$ candidates in a finite family and paying only $\log L$ in the radius.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Discover--certify auditing}
\label{alg:discover}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require A target intervention $q$, a rich auditor class, and graph--time separated discovery/certification data.
\State Fit or cache cross-fitted counterfactual scores on the discovery split.
\State Learn one or more supported auditors using \cref{eq:auditor-learning}; freeze their parameters and textual definitions.
\State Recompute nuisance functions and scores using only training data permitted for the certification split.
\State Certify each frozen auditor with \COMET, including numerator and support-mass uncertainty.
\State \Return certified violations, non-violations, and abstentions; retain the discovery score only as a search diagnostic.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The protocol separates two questions that are often confused: \emph{Can a model find a suspicious pattern?} and \emph{Does fresh dependent data support that pattern counterfactually?} A useful learned auditor should succeed on both.

\subsection{Pessimistic policy repair}
Let $V(q)$ be a counterfactual utility moment and $C_j(q)$, $j=1,\ldots,J$, be risk, cost, calibration, spillover, or fairness moments with acceptable levels $\alpha_j$. Suppose \COMET\ gives simultaneous estimates and radii over a candidate library $\Qcal$:
\begin{equation}
\abs{\widehat V(q)-V(q)}\le r_V(q),
\qquad
\abs{\widehat C_j(q)-C_j(q)}\le r_j(q).
\label{eq:sim-policy-event}
\end{equation}
We select
\begin{equation}
\widehat q\in\argmax_{q\in\Qcal}
\bigl\{\widehat V(q)-r_V(q)\bigr\}
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\widehat C_j(q)+r_j(q)\le\alpha_j
\ \forall j.
\label{eq:policy-repair}
\end{equation}
This rule is pessimistic in both directions: it optimizes a utility lower confidence bound and constrains risk upper confidence bounds. The policy library may contain the current policy, mixtures with an exploration policy, threshold or group-offset variants, and policies produced by an unconstrained learner. A continuous neural policy class can be optimized first, but only a frozen candidate library is used in the certification theorem.

\begin{proposition}[Feasibility and utility of repaired policy]
\label{prop:repair}
On the simultaneous event \cref{eq:sim-policy-event}, any solution of \cref{eq:policy-repair} satisfies $C_j(\widehat q)\le\alpha_j$ for all $j$. Moreover, for every comparator $q$ satisfying the robust margin condition $C_j(q)+2r_j(q)\le\alpha_j$ for all $j$,
\begin{equation}
    V(\widehat q)\ge V(q)-2r_V(q).
    \label{eq:repair-utility}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The margin condition is informative rather than cosmetic. A policy whose estimated constraint lies exactly at the boundary cannot be certified without additional data. As $N_{\eff}$ grows, the robustly feasible set expands and the utility penalty contracts at the heavy-tail rate. This connects statistical design choices---exploration, candidate budget, interference radius, window length---directly to deployable policy quality.

\paragraph{Sequential deployment.}
In an online system, repair is run at auditable epochs rather than after every event. A new policy is admitted only if its current-window certificates pass; exploration and propensity logging remain active after deployment. Sequential validity can be obtained by allocating confidence across epochs or replacing fixed-window bounds with e-processes, but full anytime-valid theory is left as an extension. The experiments report cumulative violations and detection delay under controlled shifts rather than claiming fixed-window intervals are automatically time-uniform.
