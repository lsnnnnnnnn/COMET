\section{Gate A: Explicit Independent-Block Certificate}
\label{app:gate-a-certificate}

This section gives the finite-sample theorem used by the initial certified
implementation. Its conditioning and independence assumptions are stronger than
those stated for the adaptive research program in
\cref{thm:uniform-comet}. They are necessary for the proof below: conditioning
on future adaptive contexts can change earlier conditional means, and
conditioning on mutually trained nuisance fits can create dependence among
evaluation scores. Neither difficulty is resolved by a temporal guard alone.
The original adaptive/cross-fitting theorem remains unproved outside the
following model.

\paragraph{Model and estimand.}
Let $\mathcal D$ be generated by an exogenous design fixed before all
evaluation randomization and, optionally, a nuisance pilot independent of
the evaluation sample conditional on that design. All statements below
condition on $\mathcal D$. There are $B$ independent evaluation
macro-blocks with $n_0$ rows each, for $N=Bn_0$ retained rows. The design fixes contexts, references
$f_u\in[0,1]$, the finite family $\Theta$ of $m\ge1$ policy--auditor pairs,
and predictions $\widehat\mu_u(s)\in[0,1]$; $|h_u|\le1$.
Within each block, a declared sufficient dependency graph for the score
vector admits a proper coloring using at most $\chi$ colors. Here
\emph{sufficient} means that disjoint vertex sets with no connecting edges
have conditionally independent score vectors, not just uncorrelated scores.

For each unit, consistency and conditional exchangeability hold under
$\mathcal D$, and the exact logging law is
$e_u(s)=\Prb(S_u=s\mid\mathcal D)$ on the same finite exposure space as
$q_u$, where $q_u(s)\ge0$ and $\sum_s q_u(s)=1$.
Require $q_u(s)>0\Rightarrow e_u(s)>0$. Define
\begin{align}
Z_u(\theta)
&=h_u\left[
 \sum_s q_u(s)\widehat\mu_u(s)-f_u
 +\frac{q_u(S_u)}{e_u(S_u)}
       \{Y_u-\widehat\mu_u(S_u)\}\right],
\label{eq:gate-a-score}\\
m_u(\theta)
&=h_u\left[\sum_s q_u(s)\E[Y_u(s)\mid\mathcal D]-f_u\right].
\end{align}
The fit is fixed given $\mathcal D$ and the exact-propensity DR identity
gives $\E[Z_u(\theta)\mid\mathcal D]=m_u(\theta)$.
Assume stable block targets:
\begin{equation}
 \frac1{n_0}\sum_{u\in b}m_u(\theta)=M_{\mathcal D}(\theta)
 \quad\text{for every }b,\theta.
\label{eq:gate-a-target}
\end{equation}
Thus $M_{\mathcal D}$ is also the raw-row average target on this fixed
design. It is not a replacement of an arbitrary adaptive realized-history
target by its unconditional average.

\begin{theorem}[Gate A independent-block certificate]
\label{thm:gate-a-clean}
Under the preceding model, fix $1<p\le2$ and known finite
$\mathcal D$-measurable envelopes $\nu_\theta$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
 \E[|Z_u(\theta)-m_u(\theta)|^p\mid\mathcal D]
 \le \nu_\theta^p
 \quad\text{for every }u,\theta.
\label{eq:gate-a-moment}
\end{equation}
For a predeclared $\alpha\in(0,1)$ set $\delta_{\rm stat}=\alpha$ and
\begin{equation}
 K=\left\lceil8\log\frac{2m}{\delta_{\rm stat}}\right\rceil,
 \qquad B\ge2K,\qquad L_{\min}=\lfloor B/K\rfloor.
\end{equation}
Partition all $B$ complete blocks deterministically into $K$ disjoint
groups with lengths $L_g\in\{\lfloor B/K\rfloor,\lceil B/K\rceil\}$.
Let $T_g(\theta)$ average the $L_g$ block means in group $g$, using its
actual length, and let $\widehat M(\theta)$ be their median (the average of
the two middle order statistics for even $K$). Define
\begin{equation}
 r_\theta=32^{1/p}\nu_\theta
       \left(\frac{\chi}{L_{\min}n_0}\right)^{(p-1)/p}.
\label{eq:gate-a-radius}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \Prb\left(
  |\widehat M(\theta)-M_{\mathcal D}(\theta)|\le r_\theta
  \text{ for all }\theta\in\Theta
  \,\middle|\,\mathcal D\right)\ge1-\alpha.
\label{eq:gate-a-coverage}
\end{equation}
The same coverage follows unconditionally by integration.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\theta$ and write $X_u=Z_u-m_u$. For each color class $C_c$,
conditional independence and the von Bahr--Esseen inequality with
constant at most $2$ give
\[
 \E\left[\left|\sum_{u\in C_c}X_u\right|^p
       \,\middle|\,\mathcal D\right]
 \le2\sum_{u\in C_c}\E[|X_u|^p\mid\mathcal D].
\]
The color sums may depend on each other. The deterministic inequality
$|\sum_c S_c|^p\le\chi^{p-1}\sum_c|S_c|^p$ nevertheless implies
\[
 \E[|\bar Z_b-M_{\mathcal D}|^p\mid\mathcal D]
 \le2\nu_\theta^p(\chi/n_0)^{p-1}.
\]
A second application of von Bahr--Esseen across the independent blocks
in each group yields
\[
 \E[|T_g-M_{\mathcal D}|^p\mid\mathcal D]
 \le4\nu_\theta^p
       \left(\frac{\chi}{L_g n_0}\right)^{p-1}
 \le4\nu_\theta^p
       \left(\frac{\chi}{L_{\min}n_0}\right)^{p-1}.
\]
If $\nu_\theta=0$, all centered scores are zero almost surely and the
claim holds directly. Otherwise Markov's inequality at
\cref{eq:gate-a-radius} makes each group's failure
probability at most $4/32=1/8$. The indicators of group failures are
conditionally independent. Hoeffding's inequality gives
\[
 \Prb\left(\#\{\text{bad groups}\}\ge K/2\mid\mathcal D\right)
 \le \exp\{-2K(1/2-1/8)^2\}
 =e^{-9K/32}\le e^{-K/8}.
\]
When fewer than $K/2$ groups fail, both middle order statistics, if there
are two, lie within the target interval, and so does the median.
A union bound over the $m$ candidates is at most
$m e^{-K/8}\le\delta_{\rm stat}/2\le\alpha$; independence across
candidates is unnecessary. All groups have the same target by
\cref{eq:gate-a-target}, even when their lengths differ. Every complete
block is used; no remainder blocks or rows are discarded.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Known bounded-reward envelope.}
For $Y_u(s)\in[0,1]$, the score in \cref{eq:gate-a-score} satisfies
$|Z_u|\le1+q_u(S_u)/e_u(S_u)$. Therefore
\begin{align}
 \E[|Z_u|^p\mid\mathcal D]
 &\le2^{p-1}\left[1+\sum_s q_u(s)^p e_u(s)^{1-p}\right],\\
 \E[|Z_u-\E(Z_u\mid\mathcal D)|^p\mid\mathcal D]
 &\le2^p\E[|Z_u|^p\mid\mathcal D].
\end{align}
The second line follows from the two-term convexity inequality and
conditional Jensen; it does not assert that centering decreases a raw
$p$th moment. Hence the valid computable choice is
\begin{equation}
 \nu_\theta^p
 =\max_u 2^{2p-1}
       \left[1+\sum_s q_u(s)^p e_u(s)^{1-p}\right].
\label{eq:gate-a-envelope}
\end{equation}
A $q_u(s)=0$ term is zero, including when $e_u(s)=0$; a state with
$q_u(s)>0=e_u(s)$ is a support failure. The bound uses the known logging
law and outcome range, not oracle response means or an empirical tail
estimate. A finite design with supported states has a finite maximum.
This does not imply a nonrandom uniform conditional envelope over a
population with arbitrarily small random propensities; the envelope and
its possible dependence on design or sample size must be reported.

\paragraph{Implemented synthetic specialization.}
Each macro-block repeats the same fixed context pattern and has a graph
consisting of disjoint $\chi$-cliques. A clique contains exact copies of
one cluster record. Logging and potential-outcome draws are independent
between clusters and between macro-blocks. The observed reward is
Bernoulli, the exposure is the binary action, the finite policy--auditor
family is frozen before evaluation, and the nuisance prediction is a
predeclared fixed function. These facts supply graph sufficiency,
stability, randomization, and conditional independence by construction.
The same argument permits a genuinely independent pilot, but not mutual
cross-fitting on evaluation blocks. The API uses
\cref{eq:gate-a-envelope} and \cref{eq:gate-a-radius}, rejects infeasible
$B<2K$, and sets nuisance radius, drift radius, and coupling failure
probability to zero. Nominal $95\%$ and $90\%$ statements use $\alpha=.05$
and $.10$, respectively, over the full frozen family at each level.

\paragraph{Coupling and other budgets.}
For a future extension, suppose that on a common probability space the
entire original scored-block vectors can be coupled to copies that are
independent conditional on $\mathcal D$, with the required marginals and
target, and with conditional mismatch probability at most
$\varepsilon_{\rm coup}$. Concentration must first be proved for the
copy law. For the corresponding failure events,
\[
 \Prb(F_{\rm original}\mid\mathcal D)
 \le \Prb(F_{\rm copy}\mid\mathcal D)
       +\Prb(\text{any mismatch}\mid\mathcal D).
\]
The copies need not remain independent after conditioning on successful
coupling. An advertised level $1-\alpha$ requires
$\delta_{\rm stat}+\varepsilon_{\rm coup}+\delta_{\rm aux}\le\alpha$,
where $\delta_{\rm aux}$ accounts for any separately proved random
envelopes. Coupling probability is not an additive numerical radius.
Nonzero nuisance and drift corrections would require proved uniform
block/group target bounds; a window-average nuisance error alone does
not control a nonlinear median. No such coupling, estimated-scale,
adaptive-fit, or unknown-drift extension is exposed by the certified API.

\paragraph{Subgroup and deployment scope.}
For binary fixed-design auditors the mass
$\pi_{\mathcal D}=N^{-1}\sum_u h_u$ is known exactly. If it is positive,
dividing a numerator interval by this mass gives the corresponding
fixed-design subgroup interval; if zero, the ratio is undefined.
A superpopulation mass requires its own sampling theorem. Positive
mass does not replace exposure support or guarantee useful precision.
The certificate is for a single predeclared evaluation window.
Pessimistic selection on its simultaneous event proves feasibility of
the same local fixed-design moments. It does not prove pathwise
long-run safety after deploying a policy that changes future contexts
or feedback.
