\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
Counterfactual auditing asks whether a policy's value or a subgroup's outcome would satisfy a declared requirement under a target exposure distribution. A point estimate cannot answer this question when rare logged exposures or correlated records make its uncertainty large. The relevant distinction is between a valid certificate and a certificate narrow enough to support a decision.

We study a finite family of counterfactual moments under a fixed exogenous evaluation design. Exposure propensities are known, evaluation macro-blocks are independent, and a sufficient dependency graph describes dependence within each block. Policies, auditors and outcome predictions are fixed before evaluation; an independent pilot may supply them. This model permits weak positive overlap and finite-$p$ score moments while keeping the conditioning needed for finite-sample inference explicit.

\COMET\ provides two separate inference routes. A graph-adjusted median of means (MOM) uses a known conditional moment envelope and a common target across blocks. Its number of groups can be chosen by an exact binomial majority bound. For bounded outcomes, a graph-aware ordinary mean instead uses a known variance upper bound and Chebyshev's inequality. Each procedure spends its own confidence budget over the whole frozen family. Neither choosing the narrower observed interval nor pooling their guarantees is justified without an additional argument.

The contribution is an explicit proof-to-computation contract for these counterfactual certificates: the target, dependence assumptions, constants, confidence budget, feasible group counts, and refusal conditions are stated together. The underlying DR identity, MOM argument, graph coloring and pessimistic selection are established tools, not new concentration principles. An exact-replication subclass supplies an expected-risk lower bound with an explicit constant; it does not establish graph-specific optimality. On a simultaneous coverage event, the same moment interface supports held-out subgroup tests and local constrained selection. Empirical discovery and repair remain separate future studies.

The frozen six-cell study evaluates coverage, width, non-vacuity and abstention under the declared model. It does not simulate feedback-adaptive histories, general network interference or learned cross-fitting. The result section will contain only verified confirmatory outputs; integration preflight observations are excluded from its coverage denominators.
