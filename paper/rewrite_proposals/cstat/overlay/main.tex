\begin{abstract}
Rare exposures and correlated observations can make counterfactual point estimates too uncertain for subgroup auditing or constraint certification. We study simultaneous inference for a finite family of counterfactual moments with known exposure propensities, independent evaluation macro-blocks, and sufficient within-block dependency graphs. Policies, auditors and nuisance predictions are fixed before evaluation or learned from an independent pilot. We give two separate certificates: a median-of-means construction with a known conditional finite-$p$ moment envelope and exact-binomial group selection, and a graph-aware mean certificate for bounded outcomes. Both have explicit finite-sample constants and applicability conditions. An expected-risk lower bound applies to an exact clique-replication subclass; a simultaneous coverage event also yields local feasibility and a robust-margin utility comparison for pessimistic policy selection. The analysis distinguishes validity from informativeness: weak overlap or heavy tails can leave a valid interval vacuous and require abstention. General adaptive feedback, mutually trained nuisance estimators and future deployment trajectories remain outside the proved scope.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Ethics Statement}
The framework is intended to diagnose and constrain counterfactual failures in deployed decision systems, but a valid certificate is conditional on the exposure mapping, logged propensities, identification assumptions, auditor family, and data quality. Misspecified interference neighborhoods or unmeasured confounding can create false assurance. Sensitive group labels should be collected and processed only with appropriate legal, organizational, and privacy safeguards. Dataset experiments will follow the licenses and intended-use conditions of the original releases. Policy repair should not be treated as a substitute for domain review, stakeholder consultation, or prospective evaluation.
